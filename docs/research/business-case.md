# Business Case Research

## Business Case:

- Read 2-3 case studies from Google Marketing Platform about development tools and tech.
  - Rail Europe accelerates page load speed by 20% - forgotten marketing tags were slowing the loading page. Use of Tag Manager helped rid of unnecessary tags and page load times improved
  - Omni Hotel rids of cookies and uses PAIR to promote relevant ads and bring more traffic for bookings

- A/B Testing:
  - A practice that optimizes user experience and improves web and mobile performance = meets the users’ needs and improves business results
  - Split testing = compares 2 or more variables for digital assets (webpage, app or email) to find which gets better outcomes, higher engagement, conversions and click-through rates, basically who stays longer on the web interface.
  - Performance metrics aer analyzed and compared
  - Common uses of A/B Testing: Web design, email campaigns, product features and ads
  - Benefits: Data-driven in decisions.
  - Little risk of putting ineffective changes
  - User experience and metrics continue to improve.
  - Why do A/B Testing? optimizes user experience and improves web and mobile performance & higher engagement, conversions and click-through rates, Better ROI.

- Use Cases:
  - What is 'Use Case'? Its a tools used to define required user interaction and is vital in the developmental life cycle phases.
  - User-oriented
  - Use case writers, team members and customers are essential in creating these cases. The developmental team puts cases together and should be viewed and known for the projects concepts.

- First Plan:
  - Preparing your first agile plan that gives a clear understanding of the project's concept
  - 1. Create Master Story List : collection of ideas/features that the users want to see - prioritizing the users' wants and needs and gives a general idea of the project. A master story list will have between 1 - 6 months worth of project efforts.
       Its common to not complete all listed items due to time and money, prioritizing the main goals are efficient to meet release deadlines (MMF) with a short timeframe.
  - 2. Size It Up: Understanding the bigger picture of all the itemized features of the project and seeing whats logical and realistic to get done.
  - 3. Prioritize: What's most important in the project? Having the client/customer make the decision of what is immediate from the business aspect of development. The client makes the ultimate decisions on what they want and dont want.
  - 4. Estimate Team's Velocity: Setting an estimated pace for the team completing tasks over tasks in-progress. Initially, its varies due to the start of planning and figuring out the how the team will go about the workflow.
    - After a few processes and meetings, the flow should be established and a more precise timeframe will be known.
    - Done = Done, all analysis, testing, design and code is completed. EVERYTHING.
  - 5. Pick Some Dates:
    - Deliver by date = when the actual product will be delivred/completed. for release
    - Deliver by feature set = working on features until they're completed before the whole project is completed.
